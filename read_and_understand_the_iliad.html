Section 2. During Reading: Read, Pause, and Think

Read one part at a time. You may read silently or aloud, but pause after each part to check whether you understand what happened.

Part 1 • Anger and honor

The Iliad by Homer is an epic poem set during the final year of the Trojan War. The story focuses on Achilles and the consequences of his anger. A conflict begins when Agamemnon takes Briseis, who had been given to Achilles as a prize of war. Achilles feels insulted and refuses to fight for the Greeks. Without their strongest warrior, the Greek army becomes weaker, allowing Hector and the Trojans to gain an advantage.

Pause and think: Part 1

What caused Achilles to stop fighting? Agamemnon took Briseis, which Achilles viewed as an insult to his honor.

Why did Achilles' decision matter to the Greek army? His absence weakened the army and gave the Trojans an advantage.

Part 2 • Patroclus enters the battle

As the Greeks begin to lose, Achilles' close friend Patroclus asks to wear Achilles' armor and lead the troops. Achilles agrees but warns him not to pursue the Trojans too far. Patroclus ignores the warning and continues toward the city walls. He faces Hector, who kills him with the help of Apollo. Patroclus' death becomes a major turning point in the story.

Pause and think: Part 2

What warning did Achilles give Patroclus? He warned him not to pursue the Trojans too far.

Why is Patroclus' death a turning point? It changes Achilles' emotions and motivates him to return to battle.

Part 3 • Revenge and its consequences

When Achilles learns of Patroclus' death, he feels grief, guilt, and rage. He sets aside his quarrel with Agamemnon and returns to battle. With new armor made by Hephaestus, Achilles becomes nearly unstoppable. He eventually kills Hector in a duel and dishonors Hector's body by dragging it behind his chariot. This action shows how deeply anger has consumed Achilles.

Pause and think: Part 3

What motivates Achilles to return to battle? His grief and desire to avenge Patroclus.

What does Achilles' treatment of Hector's body reveal? His anger has overwhelmed his judgment and sense of respect.

Part 4 • Compassion and reconciliation

Priam, the elderly king of Troy and Hector's father, enters the Greek camp and asks Achilles to return his son's body for burial. Priam reminds Achilles of his own father and the pain of losing a loved one. Moved by Priam's sorrow and courage, Achilles feels compassion and agrees. The story ends with Hector's funeral and emphasizes the shared humanity of enemies.

Pause and think: Part 4

What changes Achilles' attitude toward Priam? Priam's grief reminds Achilles of his own father and helps him feel compassion.

What does the ending emphasize? Even enemies share human feelings and deserve empathy and respect.

Section 3. Check Your Understanding

Choose the best answer. Read each question carefully and think about the meaning of the story.

1. What is the main reason Achilles refuses to fight for the Greeks?
Correct answer: B. Achilles believes that Agamemnon has dishonored him.
2. Why does Patroclus' death change the direction of the story?
Correct answer: C. Patroclus' death becomes the turning point that brings Achilles back to battle.
3. What can you infer about Achilles from his treatment of Hector's body?
Correct answer: A. His actions show how destructive uncontrolled anger can become.
4. Why does Priam's request affect Achilles?
Correct answer: B. The reminder of his father helps Achilles recognize Priam's suffering.
5. Which statement best expresses a central message of the story?
Correct answer: C. The ending highlights shared humanity, empathy, and respect.

Section 4. Think Deeper: Explain Your Ideas

Answer in complete sentences. Use details from the reading to support your explanation. There is no need to copy the passage word for word.

1. How does the quarrel between Achilles and Agamemnon reflect values of pride and honor in Greek culture?
2. In what ways does Hector's confrontation with Achilles symbolize more than just a personal battle?
3. What values are revealed when characters confront the inevitability of death as part of nature?
4. How does the natural setting of war battlefields, weather, and physical hardship challenge the warriors' endurance?
5. Can a character's defiance of societal norms in The Iliad be interpreted as a critique of cultural values?
6. What does Achilles' eventual reconciliation with Priam suggest about the resolution of internal conflict?
7. If you were to integrate all four types of conflict, what overarching message about human values does The Iliad convey to modern readers?
